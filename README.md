Space is mind-blowingly vast and full of phenomena that defy our everyday logic. Since you are interested in interesting facts, here are some of the most "alien" concepts in the universe:

1. Silence is Absolute
Sound waves need a medium (like air or water) to travel through. Because space is a vacuum, there is no atmosphere to transmit sound. This means that even if a massive star exploded right next to you, it would be completely silent.

Astronauts use radio waves to communicate because radio waves can still be transmitted in a vacuum.

2. Cold Welding
If two pieces of the same type of metal touch in space, they will permanently bond together. This is called "Cold Welding." On Earth, oxygen creates a thin layer of oxidized material on surfaces that prevents them from sticking. In the vacuum of space, there is no oxidation, so the atoms of two metal pieces have no way of knowing they are separate.

3. The Diamond Planet
There is a planet called 55 Cancri e (roughly twice the size of Earth) that scientists believe is composed largely of graphite and diamond. Because it is so close to its star and under immense pressure and heat, a third of the planet's mass could be pure diamond.

4. Space Smell
Astronauts often report that space has a distinct smell once they return from a spacewalk. They describe it as a mix of seared steak, hot metal, and welding fumes. This is likely due to high-energy vibrations in particles that cling to their suits.

5. Time Dilation
According to Einstein’s theory of relativity, gravity and speed warp time.

The closer you are to a massive object (like a Black Hole), the slower time passes for you compared to someone far away.

The faster you move, the slower time passes. If you spent a year traveling at near-light speed, you might return to Earth to find that decades have passed for everyone else.

6. The "Great Attractor"
There is a mysterious gravitational anomaly in intergalactic space known as the Great Attractor. It is so massive that it is pulling our Milky Way galaxy, and hundreds of thousands of others, toward it at a speed of about 600 kilometers per second. We can't see exactly what it is because it's hidden behind the "Zone of Avoidance" (the dusty center of our own galaxy).

7. Neutron Stars are Insanely Dense
Neutron stars are the remnants of giant stars that have collapsed. They are so dense that a single teaspoon of neutron star material would weigh about 6 billion tons (roughly the weight of Mount Everest).
